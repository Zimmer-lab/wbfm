# Draft figure captions (5 neuronal PCA modes before CCA)

Style follows the bioRxiv v1 legends (Fig. 1 and Fig. S5): bold title sentence, short noun-phrase entries per panel,
n given per entry, shared boxplot sentence at the end. Panel letters are lowercase to match the assembled figures.

**Figure 2. Low dimensional brain activity is dominated by high-level action transitions.**
**(a)** Transitions between behavioral states across freely moving datasets (n=36). Node size and percentages show
the fraction of time spent in each state; arrow width shows the transition probability between states (transitions
below 3% not shown).
**(b-d)** Example neuronal latent spaces generated by PCA (b), CCA with continuous behaviors (c), or CCA with discrete
behaviors (d), colored by behavioral state. Axis labels give the neuronal variance explained by each component.
**(e)** Cumulative variance explained of brain-wide neuronal activity by the top components of PCA, CCA, and CCA
discrete across datasets (n=36).
**(f)** Cosine similarity between the neuronal weights of each CCA component and the PCA component of the same rank,
for continuous and discrete CCA (n=36).
**(g)** Correlation of latent space generated from neuronal and behavioral data, per component, for continuous and
discrete CCA (n=36).
**(h)** Variance explained of each behavioral variable by the top neuronal components of PCA or CCA, grouped into
high-level and low-level behaviors. Colors show the median across datasets (n=36).
**(i-j)** Behavior CCA weights of the first component for continuous (i) and discrete (j) CCA (n=36).
**(k)** Neuronal CCA weights of the first component, for both continuous and discrete CCA. Left/right pairs are
combined, and only neurons identified in at least 3 datasets are shown; less confident IDs are in parentheses (n=36
freely moving datasets; see Table S1 for number per neuron).
For all CCA panels, neuronal activity was first reduced to the top 5 PCA modes (see Methods). Boxplots show the
median, interquartile range, and 1.5 times the interquartile range.

**Figure S?. Dependence of CCA on the number of neuronal PCA modes.**
**(a)** Cumulative neuronal variance explained by the top 3 components of CCA and CCA discrete, as a function of the
number of neuronal PCA modes used as input to CCA. PCA (top 3 components) is shown for reference, and the dashed line
marks the 5 modes used in Fig. 2 (n=36).
**(b)** Same as (a), but for the variance explained of the behavioral variables (continuous variables for PCA and CCA,
discrete variables for CCA discrete).
**(c-d)** Correlation of latent space generated from neuronal and behavioral data per component, for continuous (c)
or discrete (d) CCA, as a function of the number of PCA modes (n=36).
**(e-f)** Squared cosine similarity between the neuronal weights of each CCA component and each PCA mode, when using
3 (left) or 5 (right) PCA modes, for continuous (e) or discrete (f) CCA. Values are the mean across datasets (n=36);
each row sums to 1.
**(g)** Cosine similarity between the neuronal CCA weights calculated with 3 or 5 PCA modes, per dataset and
component (n=36).
**(h)** Median neuronal CCA weight per neuron calculated with 3 or 5 PCA modes, for the first (left) and second (right)
components. The dashed line shows equality, r is the Pearson correlation, and the neurons with the largest weights are
labeled.
For (a-d), lines show the mean and shading shows the standard deviation. Boxplots show the median, interquartile
range, and 1.5 times the interquartile range.

**Figure S?. Higher modes of CCA.**
**(a)** Neuronal CCA weights of the second component, for both continuous and discrete CCA. Left/right pairs are
combined, and only neurons identified in at least 6 datasets are shown (n=36).
**(b-d)** Behavior CCA weights of the second component for continuous (b) and discrete (c) CCA, and of the third
component for continuous CCA (d) (n=36).
**(e)** Cumulative fraction of neurons as a function of the variance explained per neuron by the top 2 components of
CCA (green) or CCA discrete (pink). Dashed lines mark the fraction of neurons with less than 50% variance explained.
Lines show the mean and shading shows the standard deviation (n=36).
**(f)** Variance explained of each discrete behavioral variable by the top neuronal components of CCA discrete. Colors
show the median across datasets (n=36).
For all panels, neuronal activity was first reduced to the top 5 PCA modes (see Methods). Boxplots show the median,
interquartile range, and 1.5 times the interquartile range.
